# NexOffice — Commercial Launch Playbook

## 1. Category thesis

NexOffice is not positioned as “another ERP”, “another CRM”, or “AI agents”.

The commercial category is:

**Operational control for companies that want to grow without increasing chaos.**

The product promise is:

> NexOffice gathers what is happening in the business, shows what needs attention, and puts a digital team to help execute — while the company keeps control.

Internal positioning shorthand:

> **NexOffice is the layer between the owner and operational chaos.**

## 2. The problem we sell against

The core enemy is not lack of software. It is manual coordination.

The prospect recognizes the problem when:

- the owner has to remember, chase and verify everything;
- customer context is spread across CRM, WhatsApp, email and spreadsheets;
- follow-ups are forgotten;
- accounts receivable are monitored manually;
- tasks do not have a clear owner;
- documents and pending items are hard to find;
- agenda and operation do not share context;
- the company discovers problems late;
- growth increases the amount of coordination concentrated in a few people.

## 3. Primary ICP

Initial commercial focus:

### A. Service companies
Owner still participates in sales, delivery, collections and coordination.

### B. Professional offices
Clients, deadlines, documents, tasks, agenda and finance live in different places.

### C. Commercial operations
There is a pipeline, but follow-up discipline and daily prioritization are weak.

### D. Growing small and medium businesses
More people and more tools have created more operational noise and dependence on management.

The strongest buying signal is:

> “If I stop checking, reminding or asking, things stop moving.”

## 4. What we do not lead with

Do not open communication with:

- AI;
- agents;
- ERP;
- CRM;
- integrations;
- automation jargon;
- technical architecture.

These are proof and mechanism, not the opening promise.

Lead with the operational pain the prospect already feels.

## 7. The cost-of-chaos narrative

We do not invent ROI percentages.

We show where the invisible cost appears:

- a sale that goes cold because there was no follow-up;
- a collection nobody followed;
- an hour of the owner's time spent looking for information;
- a task without an owner;
- rework caused by fragmented context;
- a decision made late;
- growth that multiplies coordination work.

Commercial phrase:

## 15. Objection handling

### “Já tenho ERP.”

NexOffice is not positioned as a replacement for every canonical system. The value is the operational layer: bringing context, priorities and actions together. Existing systems can remain sources of truth where appropriate.

### “Já tenho CRM.”

The question is not whether contacts and opportunities are stored. The question is whether the next action reliably happens and whether it is connected to the rest of the business.

### “Minha empresa é pequena.”

Small teams often feel coordination pain earlier because responsibilities overlap and the owner absorbs gaps manually. Trial is the best way to determine whether the pain is real enough to justify the product.

### “Não quero IA tomando decisões.”

Sensitive actions are designed around permissions, approval and autonomy policies. The positioning is human control, not uncontrolled automation.

### “R$ 197 é caro.”

Do not answer with fabricated ROI. Return to the operational cost the prospect already described: lost follow-up, manual collections, owner time, rework and coordination. Then invite them to validate the value during the 7-day trial.

## 17. Launch sequence

### Phase 1 — Founder network

Goal: first real users and qualitative feedback.

- direct WhatsApp outreach;
- LinkedIn founder posts;
- personal demos;
- no broad paid traffic yet.

### Phase 2 — Proof

Goal: collect real language from users.

- onboarding friction;
- which pain triggered adoption;
- which NexOffice screen created the strongest aha moment;
- which operational outcome made R$ 197 feel justified.

Use this language to improve site and ads.

### Phase 3 — Paid acquisition

Only after trial onboarding and activation are proven.

Test creative by pain, not by generic product feature.

## 18. Metrics to watch

Acquisition:

- landing → trial start;
- source by channel;
- cost per trial when paid media starts.

Activation:

- workspace created;
- first contact/opportunity;
- first task/agenda item;
- first finance record;
- first Command Center use;
- first Digital Team interaction.

Conversion:

- trial → Pix authorization;
- trial → paid;
- cancellation reason;
- time to first value.

Retention:

- weekly active workspaces;
- Command Center usage;
- number of operational areas used;
- repeated use of Digital Team;
- payment continuity.

## 19. Brand guardrails

Always:

- sell control, clarity and execution;
- use concrete business situations;
- keep the owner in control;
- distinguish the product from a generic AI wrapper;
- avoid unsupported ROI claims;
- avoid “first/only in Brazil” claims without evidence;
- do not imply every external action is autonomous;
- do not overpromise integrations that are not yet live.
